# Verification of the full-scale extension (report/report.md)

Scope: Abstract extension sentences, Sections 2.6, 3.1 (new sentences), 3.6, Section 4 "Scale and real geography" and the extension limitation bullet, Conclusion, Appendix B, Appendix C. Every number not listed below was checked against the CSVs and matches at the stated precision (Table 5 in full, all Section 3.6 ranges, strata, subzone values, doses, imported infections, design counts, 4,160/12,480, fit errors, HDB and facility counts, Census commuting figures, area and side length, seeds). The test suite was rerun and passes.

## ERROR

1. **Section 2.6 Schools bullet; Appendix B5 step 4; Figure 2 caption.** "for tertiary education the National University of Singapore (Kent Ridge) and Singapore Polytechnic campuses, both in Queenstown." The National University of Singapore (NUS) is not a site. `planning-evidence/queenstown/model/sites.csv` has two tertiary rows: ANGLO-CHINESE JUNIOR COLLEGE and Singapore Polytechnic. In `derive_queenstown.R`, `title()` produces "National University of S'pore", which does not match the filter string "National University Of S'pore". The NUS parcels are therefore dropped, and the junior college (filtered `lvl == "JUNIOR COLLEGE"`) supplies the second site. Figure 2 shows no tertiary symbol on the Kent Ridge campus. Replacement for 2.6: "and for tertiary education Anglo-Chinese Junior College and the Singapore Polytechnic campus. The National University of Singapore campus was omitted because of a subzone-name mismatch in the input derivation." In B5 step 4, replace "tertiary sites are also the National University of Singapore (Kent Ridge) and Singapore Polytechnic campuses" with "the tertiary sites are the junior college (Anglo-Chinese Junior College) and the Singapore Polytechnic campus; the National University of Singapore campus was not included." Better still, fix `derive_queenstown.R` and rerun. As it stands, all university-age students attend two sites, one of which is a junior college.

2. **Appendix B1 (Part 2 bullet), B5 step 4, B6 last row.** These say "hawker centres, community clubs and supermarkets", "and the licensed supermarkets inside it", and "Hawker centres, community clubs, supermarkets … NEA supermarket licences d_11edd…". `venues.csv` contains only 10 hawker centres and 5 community clubs, and `manifest.json` does not list the supermarket file as an input. Section 2.6 correctly says "Supermarkets were not included." Replacements: in B1, "…real school, preschool and tertiary sites, hawker centres and community clubs." In B5, "Venue sites are the existing hawker centres and the community clubs within 300 m of Queenstown." In B6, "Hawker centres, community clubs | National Environment Agency d_4a086…; People's Association d_f706… | Part 2 venue sites".

3. **Section 2.6, data currency.** "The facility and housing data are current (2024 to 2026)". `planning-evidence/queenstown/README.md` dates the preschool data to 2021 (FMEL_UPD_D) and the community clubs to 2021. The supermarket licences are dated 2024-06, HDB 2026-10 and schools 2026. Replacement: "The facility and housing data date from 2021 to 2026 rather than 2020:".

4. **Section 2.6 Design.** "sets R1 to R4 were run in the reference scenario and the open boundary, set R3 also with shuffled destinations, … giving 17 combinations in each part. … The plan was 30 populations with 50 epidemics each." In `scripts/scenarios.R`, `extension_design()` plans shuffled destinations for all of R1 to R4, which makes 20 combinations per part. Only R3 shuffled was completed (`ext_design_completed.csv`). 5. **Figure 10 caption.** "for the scenarios and parameter sets they share." Points are means over populations; bars are 95% Monte Carlo intervals."

6. **Appendix B7, Shuffled destinations.** "preserving every group's size and age composition". In `R/settings.R`, `shuffle_destinations` permutes workplace IDs among all workers and venue IDs among all individuals regardless of age. Only classes are permuted within level and age. Replacement: "Workplace memberships are permuted among workers, venue memberships among all individuals and class memberships among students of the same level and age. This preserves every group's size, and the age composition of classes, while breaking the link between home location and destination."

7. **Appendix C, steps 10 and 11.** "`Rscript scripts/run_extension.R 1 30 50`" and "`Rscript scripts/analyze_ext.R 30`" do not reproduce the reported results. The analyzed runs used the `A4_EXT_SCEN`/`A4_EXT_SETS` filters, 10-epidemic chunks in `experiment_ext_short*` and populations 21 to 30. `analyze_ext.R 30` stops with "expected … chunks" unless `A4_STOPPED` is set. ## OVERSTATED

11. **Abstract ("changed the comparison by less than 0.1 point"), Section 4 ("Real geography mattered little … less than 0.1 point in every combination") and Conclusion.** The statement holds for point estimates only (−0.07 to +0.06, `ext_geography_difference.csv`). The intervals extend to −0.13 and +0.14. The one interval that excludes zero (reference, R3: −0.07, −0.13 to −0.01) is a 21% reduction of a 0.32-point contrast. Section 4 replacement: "Real geography did not change the direction of any comparison: the point estimates of the change were −0.07 to +0.06 points, with intervals up to 0.14 points from zero. Against reference contrasts of 0.25 to 0.32 points, this is small but not negligible." Abstract replacement: "Placing the same populations on Queenstown's real geography changed the comparison by −0.07 to +0.06 points and changed no direction."

12. **Section 4, "Scale mattered more. With about 100,000 individuals, the introductions alone always produced a major outbreak … it came from lower transmission within outbreaks that occurred in both arms".** The "both arms" part is supported: `ext_p_major.csv` is 1 everywhere and `ext_contrast_distribution.csv` has 0 discordant pairs. But the original community and Part 1 differ in more than size. Imports scale with population, because the hazard is per susceptible: 376 against 19. The age and household structure is Queenstown's. Arm C gives all 8,128 doses to older adults, where the original Arm C spilled doses to younger adults. There are also 13 neighborhoods and about 23 gathering groups. "Lower transmission" is inferred, not decomposed: broad allocation lowered resident attack 1.8 to 2.3 points more (`residents: C minus B`), but direct and indirect effects were not separated. Replacement: "The original community and the full-scale populations differ in size, in the number of introductions (which scales with population: about 376 against 19), in age and household structure, and in whether Arm C could reach every older adult, so the change cannot be attributed to scale alone. At full scale, the introductions always produced a major outbreak in every arm, so broad allocation's advantage in the reference scenario no longer depended on early extinction. It arose in outbreaks that occurred under both allocations and is consistent with the lower resident attack under broad allocation (1.8 to 2.3 points), although direct and indirect effects were not separated."

13. **Abstract ("The same directions held at full scale on the real geography") and Conclusion ("The same pattern held when the model was scaled…").** Both sentences follow a list that includes vaccine protection in older adults. The extension ran no vaccine-protection scenarios, only reference, outside exposure, shuffled destinations and the cap with exposure. Abstract replacement: "At full scale on the real geography of a planning area, the reference and outside-exposure results kept their directions; the vaccine-protection scenarios were not run at full scale." Conclusion replacement: "The directions under the reference assumptions and with continued outside exposure held when the model was scaled to the whole population of the Queenstown planning area and placed on its real subzones, housing and facilities."

14. **Conclusion, "At that scale, household composition mattered more: broad allocation protected older adults in large households, and those living with children or a domestic worker, better than prioritization did."** This omits that it holds under the reference assumptions only (under exposure, O3, prioritization was ahead in every stratum) and that broad allocation was ahead in every stratum, not only these. Replacement: "At that scale and under the reference assumptions, broad allocation's advantage was largest for older adults in households of five or more and those living with their children or a domestic worker (0.36 to 0.45 points, against 0.12 for those living alone; set R3)." Similarly, in the Abstract, insert "in set R3" after "domestic worker".

15. **Section 3.6, "Shuffling destinations increased spread between neighborhoods (… 7,600 to 8,700 …) and widened broad allocation's advantage in Part 2 from +0.25 to +0.36 points, but not in Part 1."** The juxtaposition implies a link. In Part 1, cross-neighborhood infections also rose (7,755 to 8,573), yet the contrast fell (0.32 to 0.30), and the Part 2 minus Part 1 difference for shuffled destinations includes zero (+0.06, −0.03 to 0.14). Replacement: "Shuffling destinations increased cross-neighborhood infections in Arm A in both parts (Part 2: about 7,600 to 8,700 per epidemic). Broad allocation's advantage was +0.36 points with shuffled destinations against +0.25 without in Part 2, and +0.30 against +0.32 in Part 1, so the change in spread did not translate consistently into the comparison."

16. **Section 4, "(Halloran et al., 2008), as expected when the question is not itself spatial."** The gloss is not a finding of the cited paper. Replacement: "…in the same setting (Halloran et al., 2008)." Optionally add, as the authors' own reasoning: "The allocation question here is not itself spatial."

17. **Section 3.6, "0.096 for older adults' living arrangements, close to the 0.10 limit because the national living arrangements had to fit Queenstown's smaller households."** The cause is asserted without a check. Replace "because" with "probably because".

18. **Section 2.6 Floor-area bullet and Section 3.6 strata.** "Floor area per occupant therefore varies with housing type and location" and "0.34 in the lowest third of floor area per occupant against 0.14 in the highest". Floor area has no effect on transmission at full scale (`gamma = 0`; B7 "Living space (original community only)"). The floor-area gradient therefore reflects correlated household size and dwelling type. Add to 2.6: "Floor area does not affect transmission at full scale (the living-space weight is zero); it is used only to define household strata." Add to 3.6 after the floor-area numbers: "(floor area has no direct effect in these runs, so this gradient reflects household size)".

19. **Section 4 Scalability, "so cost grows roughly in proportion to population size."** Population grows about 19-fold (5,257 to about 101,800) while time grows about 11 to 12-fold (1.3 to 14 to 16 s). Replacement: "so cost grew somewhat less than in proportion to population size (about 12-fold for a 19-fold larger population)."

20. **Section 3.1, "epidemics rerun on the original community reproduced the stored main-experiment results exactly."** No log, test or output in the project documents this rerun. Either add the artifact (for example a comparison log in `results/summary/`) and cite it, or remove the sentence.
