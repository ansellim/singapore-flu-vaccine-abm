# Household and model evidence notes

Reviewed November 18 to 19, 2026. These notes consolidate the Sol household scout's interim findings and the supervising agent's direct checks. The scout's final report did not complete. The parent inspected the retained sources beyond their abstracts. This is a targeted narrative review, not a systematic review. No model was developed or run.

## Household exposure

**Petrie et al., 2013.** Influenza Transmission in a Cohort of Households with Children: 2010-2011. [DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0075339](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0075339), PMID 24086511. Read publisher Methods, Results, and Discussion. Prospective Michigan cohort: 328 households, eligibility at least four members and two children. Persons per room measured crowding; transmission had no clear association with that measure or household size. Symptom-triggered sampling omitted asymptomatic infections, and the restricted population limits transportability. This is not evidence of equivalence or a Singapore floor-area coefficient. Use to justify retaining a no-independent-space-effect scenario and matching calibration endpoints to the study's ascertainment.

**Tsang et al., 2023.** Reconstructing household transmission dynamics to estimate the infectiousness of asymptomatic influenza virus infections. [DOI 10.1073/pnas.2304750120](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2304750120), PMID 37549267. Parent read the three-page article including Results, Discussion, and Methods; supplementary methods were not independently read. Hong Kong household inference combines serology, virology, and symptoms. Relative infectiousness uncertainty spans equal infectiousness; behavior and biology are not separable in its estimate. Recently vaccinated participants were excluded, and pandemic H1N1 results need not describe seasonal strains. Published data/code are linked in the article. Do not multiply its net relative-infectiousness estimate by additional behavioral reductions without checking double counting.

## Singapore contact evidence

**Jiang et al., 2018.** Infectious disease transmission: survey of contacts between hospital-based healthcare workers and working adults from the general population. [DOI 10.1016/j.jhin.2017.10.020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhin.2017.10.020), PMID 29097147. Parent inspected extracted full-text Methods, Results, and Discussion from PMC7114670. The community comparison uses 1,028 adults aged 20 to 64 and selects working days, choosing the higher-work-contact diary when two qualify. This selection supports comparisons with healthcare workers, not an unbiased population-wide daily average. Repeated contacts matter; paper diaries may miss short encounters and overstate duration. Children and retired older adults require other evidence or explicit assumptions. No individual Singapore contact dataset has been acquired or requested.

## Modeling and projected contacts

**Prem et al., 2021, corrected 2024.** [DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009098](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009098), PMID 34310590. Parent read Methods, Results, and Discussion. Country-specific setting matrices are projected from demographic and survey information, not measured Singapore transmission networks. Use as age/setting mixing inputs with local plausibility checks, retaining uncertainty in oldest ages. Published data/code links are available in the article.

**Prem et al., 2024 correction.** [DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012454](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012454). Parent read complete notice. It corrects household-age adjustment, home visitors, and other-setting/school/work calculations. Use corrected data; distinguish co-residents from visitors when adding explicit households. Do not silently extrapolate the terminal age band to the oldest adults without sensitivity analysis.

**Chao et al., 2010, FluTE.** [DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000656](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000656). Parent read model specification, Results, and Discussion. It supplies precedent for recurring household/daytime settings, timed vaccination, and distinct vaccine effects on infection, illness, and infectiousness. Its foreign population and historical calibration cannot be transferred wholesale. Its two-week protection development is a modeling precedent, not a new Singapore biological estimate.

## Boundaries on inference

The evidence supports an explicit household/contact model and uncertainty analysis. It does not identify a Singapore-specific causal floor-area effect, contemporary age-specific vaccine protection against acquisition, or the complete joint distribution of household residents and dwelling characteristics. Public statistical cross-tabulations can constrain the synthetic population; missing associations must remain stated assumptions. The completed Astra audits independently identified further population-denominator and spatial-mechanism issues, recorded in their separate reports.
