Scope: Abstract (full-scale paragraphs and final paragraph), Section 2.6 with Table 2 and Figure 2, Section 3.6 with Table 5 and Figures 10 and 11, Discussion "Scale and real geography", the full-scale limitation bullet, Space / Population size / Scalability, Conclusion, Appendix B5 to B7, Appendix C items 8 to 11. Everything not listed checked out. That includes every other Table 5 cell; the calibration-check ranges; the 371 vs 19 introductions; the 3 of 7,040 epidemics that were not major outbreaks; the averted ratios (73% to 79%, 1.06 to 1.19); B minus A and C minus A; doses (8,129 and 1,369); per-100-dose ranges other than item 3; the subzone ranges and strata values; the fit errors; the 95,957 to 95,973 residents and 5,852 to 5,860 domestic workers; the 242 blocks with 34,920 flats and the 14 blocks with 2,409 flats; the 9 primary and 12 secondary schools and 3 tertiary sites; the 10, 5 and 14 venues; the 21.7 km² area and 4.66 km side; the 131,782 and 48,300 employed residents; the 22 combinations, 7,040 epidemics and 21,120 runs; the seeds; the t distribution with 15 degrees of freedom; and the interval half-widths compared with the original community. I found no number taken from ext_v1. No em or en dashes, no "agent" for people and no British spellings outside reference titles.

1. **ERROR: Table 5, "Older-adult protection 10%" row, Part 2 minus Part 1.** The table gives −0.06 (−0.13 to 0.02). `ext_geography_difference.csv` weak_older_ve R3 has an estimate of −0.000547, which is −0.0547 points and rounds to −0.05. Replacement: "−0.05 (−0.13 to 0.02)".

2. **ERROR: the range of the geography effect, "−0.06 to +0.05 points".** It appears in the Abstract ("changed the comparison by −0.06 to +0.05 points"), Section 3.6 "Effect of real geography", the Discussion "Scale and real geography" and the Conclusion. The minimum of the 22 differences is −0.0547 (weak_older_ve) and the next is −0.053 (reference R2), so the range is −0.05 to +0.05 (maximum +0.051, reference R1). Replacement in all four places: "−0.05 to +0.05 points".

3. **ERROR: Table 5, "Older-adult protection 40%" row, Part 2 minus Part 1.** The table gives −0.05 (−0.10 to 0.01). The file has −0.0445 (−0.103 to 0.014). Replacement: "−0.04 (−0.10 to 0.01)".

4. **ERROR: Section 3.6 "Whole population", "against 7 to 9 and 6 to 9 for prioritization".** Resident infections averted per 100 doses for Arm C in the reference scenario run from 7.51 (square) to 8.80 (Queenstown), so 8 to 9. The outside-exposure range (5.61 to 9.20, so 6 to 9) is correct. Replacement: "against 8 to 9 and 6 to 9 for prioritization".

5. **OVERSTATED: the claim that real geography changed no direction.** It appears in the Abstract ("and changed no direction"), the Discussion ("Real geography did not change the direction of any comparison") and the Conclusion ("The directions ... held"). In the older-adult protection 40% scenario the point estimate changes sign: Part 1 is +0.021 and Part 2 is −0.024 (`ext_contrasts.csv`), although both intervals include zero. Abstract replacement: "and reversed no comparison whose interval excluded zero". Discussion replacement: "Real geography reversed no comparison whose interval excluded zero:".

6. **OVERSTATED: "equivalent".** It appears in the Abstract ("the allocations were equivalent"), Section 3.6 ("the two allocations were equivalent at full scale") and the Discussion ("were equivalent at full scale"). No equivalence margin or test was prespecified, and the Part 2 interval runs from −0.08 to +0.04. Replacement: "indistinguishable (+0.02 and −0.02 points, intervals including zero)" in Section 3.6, and "the allocations did not differ" in the Abstract and Discussion.

7. **ERROR (input, method statement): preschool sites.** Section 2.6 ("the 129 preschools within 500 m") and Table 2 ("Same counts as Part 2") make the claim. Appendix B5 makes the same claim for Part 1 ("the same numbers of ... sites"). `sites.csv` has 129 preschool rows but only 99 distinct coordinates. At least 18 rows are exact duplicates of name and location, from truncated ECDA name variants such as "PCF Sparkletots Preschool @ Buona Vista Blk 4 (DS" and "(DS)". Because pupils go to the nearest site (`nearest_site`, ties go to the first), at most 99 preschool sites receive pupils in Part 2. In Part 1, 129 independent random sites are placed, so the two parts do not have the same effective number of preschool sites. The fix is to deduplicate in `derive_queenstown.R` and rerun. Text replacement if not rerun: "the 129 preschool records within 500 m (at 99 distinct locations, because some centers appear more than once in the source; Part 1 placed 129 sites)".

8. **OVERSTATED: Section 2.6, "two full-scale geographies that share their populations and differ only in where people live and where they go".** Floor area is also assigned differently (by household size in Part 1, by dwelling type in Part 2; see Table 2). Floor area affects household strata but not transmission. Replacement: "...differ only in where people live and where they go, and in how floor area is assigned (which defines household strata but does not affect transmission) (Table 2)."

9. **OVERSTATED (minor): Section 2.6 "Address" and Appendix B5 Part 2 step 2, "placed at a random point on a private residential parcel of its kind in its subzone".** `private-points.csv` has no points in the National University of Singapore subzone (240 condominium residents in the Census), so those households fall back to any condominium point in Queenstown. Dover, Margaret Drive, Queensway and One North have landed residents (40, 20, 20 and 80) but only condominium points, so those households fall back to condominium parcels (`queenstown_layout`). Add: "; where a subzone had no parcel of that kind, any private parcel in the subzone was used, and where it had none, any parcel of that kind in Queenstown".

10. **ERROR (minor, consistency): Section 2.6 and Appendix B5 step 3, "37,053 households".** 37,053 is the published total, but the household-size cells in `household-size.csv` sum to 37,052, and every synthesized population has 37,052 households (Section 3.6 and `world_fit_ext.csv`). Replacement: "37,052 households (the sum of the Census 2020 household-size cells; published total 37,053)".
